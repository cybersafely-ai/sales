Parent Affiliate Program

Social media creators share a personal link to the app, and individual parents sign up through it

For: Torino Fitzgerald, Nick, Zoey From: Giancarlo Zaccardelli Date: October 27, 2026 Status: Draft for discussion

The ask: approve a 60-day pilot with 10–15 parent-focused creators. Each gets a personal link, a code and a QR code. We pay only when a family they referred activates. The pilot reuses the referral-code model we already run for schools, so most of the plumbing exists. If we approve it by Nov 6, creators can be posting by Dec 1, ahead of the holidays when many children get new phones.

1. Why we should do this

  • It adds a second route to families. Today, almost every family reaches us through a principal who forwards our message. That works, but it is slow. On Oct 14 the Lead Manager showed 372 Missouri schools tracked: 73 contacted and 1 engaged. A parent can only sign up after their principal acts. Affiliates reach parents directly, in any state and at any time of year.
  • Parents trust other parents. Parents make the buying decision, and they already follow creators who talk about parenting, screen time and online safety. A recommendation from someone they follow lands better than a newsletter item.
  • We pay for results only. There are no upfront fees. Rewards are paid when a family activates (child added and keyboard enabled), not at sign-up. That puts cost directly behind our activation target (Outcome 03) rather than empty sign-ups.
  • It feeds our own social channels. With permission, creator posts can be reshared on CyberSafely accounts, which helps the follower and engagement targets (Outcome 04).
  • Most of the tools exist. Codes and QR codes already attribute sign-ups to schools in the staff portal, and the Lead Manager already runs a recruit-and-track pipeline. Affiliates are one more kind of code.

Who we would recruit

Creator typeWhy they fit
Parent creators (5k–50k followers)Smaller accounts usually get more engagement and trust than large ones, and they cost less. They are the core of the pilot.
Online-safety educators, counselors, therapistsCredible voices on cyberbullying and digital wellbeing, whose audience is already looking for help.

Our own customer parentsAmbassadors with real experience of the product. They are the easiest to recruit and the most authentic.

2. How we could reward affiliates

All amounts and percentages are for leadership to set. None are approved.

OptionHow it worksTrade-off
A. Flat reward per activated familyA fixed cash amount for each referred family that activates and stays past the refund window.The simplest to explain, track and pay. It doesn't reward long-term retention.
B. Recurring revenue shareA percentage of each subscription, every year the family stays. This mirrors how schools already earn from the families they bring in.Rewards retention (Outcome 03). Payouts are smaller at first and need longer tracking.
C. Tiers and perksA free subscription for the creator's own family when they join, then a higher rate or bonus at milestones (for example 10, 25 and 50 activated families).Creators who use the product post more honestly. Milestones keep the best ones engaged.
D. Give-back choiceThe creator can send their reward to a school of their choice, which builds on the school-fundraiser story.Strong for parent and educator creators. Keep it a school, not the Foundation (separate entity).

Recommendation for the pilot: A + the free family subscription from C. It is easy to launch and to measure. After 60 days, offer B to the top performers. Also consider a small discount for the referred family (two-sided offers usually convert better), but it needs pricing approval.

Payout rules for every option: pay on activation, not sign-up. Hold rewards 30 days (refund window), pay monthly, and take rewards back on refunds. No self-referrals, no bidding on our brand name in search ads, and no listings on coupon sites.

Build or buy

For the pilot I recommend buying an off-the-shelf affiliate platform that connects directly to our billing system. Rewardful, FirstPromoter, Tolt and PartnerStack all cover links, tracking, the portal and payouts. Setup is mainly a website script plus a billing connection, with low monthly pricing still to be quoted. Building our own only makes sense if the program grows past the pilot.

Key question for engineering: where does a parent sign up and pay, on the website or inside the app? On the web (the current onboarding flow starts with a code and billing), the setup above is simple. If sign-up happens after an app-store install, the link is lost in the store. We would then need a mobile attribution tool (for example Branch or AppsFlyer, which use deferred deep links) or a required "referral code" field in the app. We also need to confirm which billing system we use (Stripe or another).

Privacy

  • Affiliates see counts only: never parent names, emails or anything about a child.
  • The tracking cookie sits only on our parent-facing landing page and is disclosed in the privacy policy. Creators must speak to parents, never to children.

4. Guardrails

  • FTC disclosure. Every post must say it is paid or an affiliate link (for example #ad). This is written into the affiliate agreement.
  • Approved claims only. Creators get a creator kit with approved product claims, sample captions and do's and don'ts. Each creator's first post is reviewed before it goes live. Blocker: our approved-claims list is not finalized yet.
  • Correct entity. CyberSafely.ai runs and pays the program. The CyberSafely Foundation is a separate nonprofit and is not part of it.
  • Written agreement. Reward terms, payout timing, brand rules and termination, reviewed by whoever handles legal.
  • Separate from the Sales Partnership Program. That program serves sales partners. This one serves parent-facing creators.

6. Decisions needed

  1. Go or no-go on a 60-day pilot with 10–15 creators.
  2. Reward model and amounts (recommended: A plus a free family subscription), and whether referred families get a discount.
  3. Budget cap for rewards and the platform's monthly fee.
  4. Engineering answers: where sign-up and payment happen, which billing system we use, and who connects the activation event.
  5. Approved claims for the creator kit.
  6. Owners: I can run creator recruiting, the Lead Manager list, the creator kit and weekly reporting. Platform setup, the agreement and payouts need owners.
  7. School credit: when a parent signs up through a creator, should their child's school still earn its share?
